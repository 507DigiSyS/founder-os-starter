# Elon Musk

> A summary of this person's public teachings, written in our own words. It is not written or endorsed by them. Check the sources before you quote anything.

## Who they are

Elon Musk runs SpaceX and Tesla, two companies that build rockets, electric cars and batteries at large scale. He also founded or leads xAI, Neuralink and The Boring Company, and owns the social network X. People study how he works because his teams have cut costs in fields where most experts said costs could not come down. This file covers his business and engineering lessons only.

## Use this expert when

- Your costs feel too high and you want to know why
- A process has too many steps, forms, or handoffs
- You want to cut a plan down to what really matters
- You are about to automate something (software, AI, a robot) and want to check first
- You set goals and want to know if they are big enough, or just copied from others
- You are hiring and want to judge people on what they have actually done

## Core frameworks

### First principles thinking

- **What it is:** Break a problem down to the basic facts you know are true, then build your answer up from there. Do not just copy what others have done before.
- **How to use it:**
  1. Write down the thing everyone "knows" (for example, "this always costs a lot").
  2. Break it into its raw parts. What is it really made of?
  3. Price or measure each part on its own.
  4. Ask what it would take to put the parts together in a smarter way.
- **Source:** Musk interview clip "Elon Musk: First Principles" (battery example: he priced the raw metals in a battery and found them far cheaper than a finished battery pack).

### The 5 step process ("the algorithm")

- **What it is:** Five steps to improve any product or process. The order matters.
- **How to use it:**
  1. **Make the requirements less dumb.** Question every rule. Each rule should have a person's name on it, not just a department. Rules from smart people are risky because no one questions them.
  2. **Delete the part or step.** Cut anything you can. If you never have to add anything back (he says about 10% of the time), you did not cut enough.
  3. **Simplify and improve.** Only now make what is left better. The common mistake is polishing something that should not exist.
  4. **Speed up.** Make the cycle faster, but only after steps 1 to 3.
  5. **Automate.** Do this last.
- **Source:** Everyday Astronaut Starbase tour (2021); Walter Isaacson's biography *Elon Musk* (2023).

### The idiot index

- **What it is:** The cost of a finished product divided by the cost of its raw materials. A high number means waste in the design or the way it is made.
- **How to use it:**
  1. Pick a product or service you sell or buy.
  2. Add up what the raw inputs really cost.
  3. Divide the full price by that number.
  4. Start with the item that has the highest ratio. That is where the savings hide.
- **Source:** Isaacson's biography. Musk found rockets cost at least 50 times their raw materials, which led him to build rockets himself.

### The factory is the product ("the machine that makes the machine")

- **What it is:** How you make and deliver a thing can matter as much as the thing. Treat your process like a product you design and improve.
- **How to use it:**
  1. Map how your product or service gets made and delivered, step by step.
  2. Look for wasted space, wasted time and waiting.
  3. Set a target for how much faster or cheaper the process could be.
- **Source:** Tesla 2016 shareholder meeting, reported by Electrek.

### Start high, then go lower (the Tesla master plan)

- **What it is:** Launch a pricey, low volume product first. Use the money and learning from it to build cheaper, higher volume products.
- **How to use it:**
  1. Find the buyers who will pay most for the first version.
  2. Use that cash to fund a cheaper second version.
  3. Repeat until you reach the mass market.
- **Source:** "The Secret Tesla Motors Master Plan" blog post (2006).

## Key principles

- Every requirement is a suggestion until you have questioned it. Physics is the only fixed rule.
- Delete before you improve. Improve before you speed up. Speed up before you automate.
- Every rule needs an owner with a name, so you can ask them why it exists.
- Speed matters. He has called a strong sense of urgency an operating rule for his teams.
- Iterate fast. Build, test, learn, fix. When nobody can get hurt, testing until it breaks is fine.
- It is okay to be wrong. It is not okay to be wrong and sure of yourself.
- Judge people by what they have done, not by their resume.

## Mindset

- **On risk:** If something is important enough, try it even if the odds say it may fail. He told *60 Minutes* in 2012 that he would have to be insane to think the odds were in his favor when he started SpaceX.
- **On cost:** "That's how it has always been" is not a reason. Past prices do not set future prices.
- **On goals:** Pick goals that are bold enough to force new ideas. Deadlines are meant to push people, though deadlines that break the laws of physics hurt morale.
- **On mistakes:** Admit them in public and fix them. In 2018 he said too much automation at Tesla was his mistake, and that humans are underrated.
- **On leaders:** Technical leaders should stay close to the real work and the real problems.

## Strategies and plays

- **Owner on every rule.** Example: "Every invoice needs two sign offs." Who asked for that? Ask them if it is still needed.
- **The delete pass.** Example: Cut 3 steps from your onboarding. If none come back, cut more next time.
- **Idiot index your offer.** Example: A $500 service where the real inputs cost $50 has a ratio of 10. Ask where the other $450 of cost or effort goes.
- **Build it yourself when parts are overpriced.** Example: SpaceX made many parts in house instead of buying them. For you, this may mean an in house template instead of a costly vendor.
- **Fund the cheap version with the premium one.** Example: Sell a high touch premium package first, then use what you learn to build a lower cost version.
- **Do it by hand first.** Example: Run a process manually until it works well, then automate it. Automating a broken step just makes broken work faster.
- **Hire on proof.** Example: Ask each candidate for 1 to 3 short bullet points of real, hard things they have done.

## Tools they use or recommend

Musk rarely names business software. Most of what he recommends is a way of thinking or a book. Known items:

- **The idiot index:** a simple ratio you can put in any spreadsheet. See the Isaacson biography.
- **A cost spreadsheet:** Isaacson describes Musk building a spreadsheet of material costs to decide that SpaceX could build its own rocket.
- **Book: *Structures: Or Why Things Don't Fall Down* by J.E. Gordon.** He has called it a good starter book on how buildings and machines hold up. (Source: Evannex roundup of his book picks.)
- **Book: *Ignition!* by John D. Clark.** A history of liquid rocket fuels that shows up on lists of books Musk has recommended. (Source: Evannex roundup.)
- **Book: *Zero to One* by Peter Thiel.** He has praised it as showing how Thiel built breakthrough companies. (Source: Evannex roundup.)
- **Hiring screen:** ask for "evidence of exceptional ability," in 1 to 3 bullet points. (Source: Entrepreneur, 2026, on a podcast with John Collison and Dwarkesh Patel.)

## Questions they would ask you

Use these to pressure test a plan.

1. Who exactly made this requirement? What is their name?
2. What happens if we just delete this step?
3. What does this really cost to make, in raw inputs? What is the ratio to our price?
4. Are you polishing something that should not exist?
5. Why will this take 6 months? What would it take to do it in 6 weeks?
6. What do the laws of physics (or the hard facts) say is possible here? What is just habit?
7. Are you about to automate a process that still has bugs?
8. What is the boldest version of this goal? Why not go for that?
9. What hard thing has this person actually done? Show me the proof.
10. Did anything you cut have to come back? If not, did you cut enough?

## Watch out

- **Small business is not a rocket factory.** The idiot index fits physical goods best. For a service, your "raw inputs" include time, skill and trust, which are harder to price.
- **Deleting can go too far.** The 10% add back rule means you should expect to cut some things you need. Do not delete safety steps, legal steps or anything customers rely on.
- **Impossible deadlines have a cost.** Musk's own timelines are often missed. Bold goals help. Fake deadlines can burn out a small team.
- **His own automation lesson.** Tesla over automated a factory and had to pull it back. Follow the order: automate last.
- **Hard work culture is not for every team.** Reports describe very long hours and a harsh style at his companies. Copy the method, not the pressure.
- **Huge risk is not a template.** He has bet very large sums on long odds. Most founders cannot afford to lose that way. Size your bets to what you can lose.
- **Secondary sources vary.** Many online summaries of "the algorithm" add details he did not say. Check the original video or the Isaacson book.
