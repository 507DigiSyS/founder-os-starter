# Alex Hormozi

> A summary of this person's public teachings, written in our own words. It is not written or endorsed by them. Check the sources before you quote anything.

## Who they are

Alex Hormozi is a business owner and author. He runs Acquisition.com with his wife, Leila Hormozi. It is a business education company and a group of companies they own a part of.

He wrote the books *$100M Offers*, *$100M Leads* and *$100M Money Models*. Acquisition.com says the first two have each sold over 1 million copies. Before that, he and Leila built and sold the companies Gym Launch, Prestige Labs and ALAN. He also hosts a podcast called *The Game* and posts often on YouTube.

## Use this expert when

- Your offer is not selling, or people say "too expensive"
- You are setting or raising your price
- You need more leads and do not know where to start
- You want each customer to spend more over time (upsells, memberships)
- You want to know what to fix first as the business grows
- You are writing or fixing a sales call script

## Core frameworks

### The Value Equation

- **What it is:** A way to judge how much your offer is worth to a buyer. Value goes up with (1) the dream outcome and (2) how sure they are it will work. Value goes down with (3) how long it takes and (4) how much effort and pain it costs them.
- **How to use it:**
  1. Write the buyer's dream result in their words.
  2. Add proof so they believe it will work (reviews, results, a guarantee).
  3. Find ways to get them a fast first win.
  4. Remove work from their side. Do more of it for them.
- **Source:** *$100M Offers*, and the free Offers course on Acquisition.com

### The Grand Slam Offer

- **What it is:** An offer so different and so full of value that buyers cannot compare it to anyone else's. You stop competing on price.
- **How to use it:**
  1. List every problem the buyer hits on the way to their dream result.
  2. Turn each problem into a solution, and pick how you will deliver it (done for you, group, one on one, a tool).
  3. Cut the costly, low value parts. Keep the high value, low cost parts (he calls this "trim and stack").
  4. Add bonuses, a guarantee, and a real reason to act now (limited spots or a deadline).
- **Source:** *$100M Offers*

### Picking a market

- **What it is:** A good offer in a bad market still struggles. Look for buyers who have strong pain, can afford to pay, are easy to find in groups, and are in a market that is growing.
- **How to use it:** Score your customer group on those four things. If one is weak, pick a narrower group.
- **Source:** *$100M Offers* (summary by EcomCrew)

### The Core Four (getting leads)

- **What it is:** The four ways you can tell people about your offer. Two are one to one and two are one to many:
  - Warm outreach: message people who already know you
  - Content: post free, useful things for anyone
  - Cold outreach: message strangers, one at a time
  - Paid ads: pay to show your offer to strangers at scale
- **How to use it:**
  1. Start with warm outreach. It is free and the trust is already there.
  2. Get good at one more channel before you add another.
  3. Do a lot of it, every day, and track the numbers.
- **Source:** *$100M Leads* (also read aloud as audiobook chapters on *The Game* podcast)

### Lead getters

- **What it is:** Other people who bring you leads: customers (referrals), employees, agencies and affiliates (partners paid per sale).
- **How to use it:** Only add them after one channel already works for you. They make what works bigger. They do not fix what does not work.
- **Source:** *$100M Leads* and the free Leads course

### Lead magnets

- **What it is:** A free thing you give in exchange for contact info. It solves one small, clear part of a bigger problem. The paid offer solves the rest.
- **How to use it:** Make it fast to use and truly useful. A tool or a short service often beats plain information.
- **Source:** *$100M Leads* (notes by Bryce Watson), and the "Lead Magnet Mastery" lesson in the free Leads course

### More, Better, New

- **What it is:** A rule for growth. First do **more** of what works. Then make it **better**. Only then try something **new**.
- **How to use it:** Before you start a new channel or product, ask if you have pushed the current one as far as it can go.
- **Source:** *$100M Leads* and the free Leads course

### Money Models

- **What it is:** A planned order of offers that makes more money from each customer. There are four types:
  - Attraction offer: a way to get new customers in the door
  - Upsell: a bigger or better option offered after they say yes
  - Downsell: a smaller or easier option if they say no
  - Continuity: something they pay for again and again (a membership or plan)
- **How to use it:**
  1. Know what it costs to get one customer.
  2. Design your offers so a new customer's profit covers that cost fast, ideally in the first 30 days (he calls this client financed acquisition, CFA).
  3. Then use the profit to get more customers.
- **Source:** *$100M Money Models*, and the free Money Models course

### CLOSER (sales calls)

- **What it is:** A six step sales call outline: Clarify why they are here, Label their problem, Overview what they tried before, Sell the result (not the process), Explain away their concerns, Reinforce the decision after they buy.
- **How to use it:** Ask questions most of the call. Repeat their problem back to them clearly. Treat a price objection as a sign you did not show enough value.
- **Source:** *The Game* podcast, episode "I Taught 115+ Salesmen My Closing Framework"

### The Scaling Roadmap

- **What it is:** A list of business stages, from the first sale up to a large company. Each stage has one main thing holding you back.
- **How to use it:** Find your stage. Fix that one thing. Ignore the problems of later stages for now.
- **Source:** Free Scaling course on Acquisition.com (stages run from Improvise to Capitalize)

## Key principles

- If people say no, the offer is usually the problem, not the buyer.
- Do not compete on price. Make the offer so good that price matters less.
- Charging more lets you serve buyers better, and buyers who pay more often try harder.
- Pick one thing and get very good at it before adding more.
- Volume wins. Most people do far too little outreach.
- Track your numbers: cost to get a customer, profit per customer, and how fast you earn it back.
- Give away real value for free. Sell the full result.

## Mindset

- Treat business as skills you can learn, not luck or talent.
- Expect to be bad at first. Do the reps anyway.
- Focus on the one biggest problem right now.
- Think long term. Small daily work adds up over years.

## Strategies and plays

- **Add a guarantee:** take on some of the risk so the buyer feels safe. Example: "Get your first result in 30 days, or we keep working for free."
- **Stack named bonuses:** list each bonus with its own name and what it solves.
- **Use real limits:** limited spots per month, or a real deadline, so people act now.
- **Warm list blast:** message everyone you already know, tell them what you do, and ask who they know who needs it.
- **Downsell with a payment plan:** if they say no to the full price, offer the same thing split into smaller payments.
- **Turn one time buyers into members:** offer an ongoing plan right after the first win.

## Questions they would ask you

1. What is the dream outcome your buyer wants, in their own words?
2. Why should they believe it will work for them? What proof do you have?
3. How fast do they get their first win?
4. What could you do for them so they do less work?
5. If you doubled your price, what would you add to make it worth it?
6. Which one lead channel are you doing every day, and how many times a day?
7. What does it cost you to get one customer, and how fast do you earn it back?
8. What do you sell them after the first purchase?
9. What is the one thing holding the business back right now?

## Watch out

- **Built for certain businesses.** Much of it comes from gyms, coaching and service businesses. Some ideas fit software, stores or local trades less well.
- **Big guarantees can hurt.** Only promise what you can deliver and afford. A guarantee you cannot keep damages trust.
- **Fake limits are a bad idea.** Scarcity and deadlines must be real. Made up ones can break trust and may break advertising laws.
- **Higher prices need higher value.** Raising a price without improving the offer just loses buyers.
- **Upsells can annoy people.** Too many offers stacked on top of each other can feel pushy. Keep it fair and useful.
- **Not a plan for every stage.** Advice for a company with a big team may not fit a one person business. Use the Scaling Roadmap to find advice for your stage.
- **Summaries vary.** Many free online summaries of his books disagree on details. Check his books or free courses before you quote him.
