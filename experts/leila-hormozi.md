# Leila Hormozi

> A summary of this person's public teachings, written in our own words. It is not written or endorsed by them. Check the sources before you quote anything.

## Who they are

Leila Hormozi co-founded Acquisition.com with Alex Hormozi in 2021. The firm invests in and advises other businesses. She has served as its CEO, and the company site now lists her as co-founder, managing partner, and chairwoman. Before that, she built Gym Launch with Alex, started the supplement company Prestige Labs, and built the software company ALAN. Gym Launch and Prestige Labs were sold in 2021, and ALAN was sold to a separate buyer. She hosts the podcast "Build with Leila Hormozi" and writes a free newsletter, Leila's Letters, made of memos she sends her team. People listen to her for hiring, management, and building teams that can run without the founder.

## Use this expert when

- You are about to hire someone and want a clear role and a fast, fair process
- Your team is busy but results are not showing up
- You need to give hard feedback, or decide if someone should stay
- You are the bottleneck and every decision still goes through you
- You want people to see a career path, so good people stay
- You keep adding tools, steps, or people, and things only get messier

## Core frameworks

### The Accountability Formula

- **What it is:** Expectations plus measurement, multiplied by feedback, gives you accountability. She calls feedback the multiplier, the part that can amplify accountability.
- **How to use it:**
  1. Write down what "good" looks like for the role or project (expectations)
  2. Pick a simple way to score it, like a 1 to 10 scale with what it takes to reach the next number (measurement)
  3. Give clear, frequent feedback in one on one meetings (private check ins with each person), reviews, and quick messages (feedback)
  4. If results slip, check which of the three parts is missing before blaming the person
- **Source:** Young and Profiting podcast, episode 203

### Hire for traits, teach the skills

- **What it is:** Hire people with good natural traits, like attitude and emotional skills, then train the technical part. She credits this idea to the Ritz-Carlton hotel chain.
- **How to use it:**
  1. List the traits the role truly needs (for example, calm under stress, cares about customers)
  2. Separate them from skills you can teach in a few weeks
  3. Screen hard for traits, and go easier on missing skills
- **Source:** Fortune, January 2026

### Three traits of strong leaders

- **What it is:** She looks for three traits: industrious (works hard on the right things), enthusiastic (keeps energy on hard problems), and competitive greatness (likes hard challenges and moves toward them).
- **How to use it:**
  1. Turn each trait into one interview question
  2. Ask for a real past example, not a "what would you do" answer
  3. Be wary of leadership candidates driven mainly by money. She says the best leaders are not motivated by money.
- **Source:** Young and Profiting podcast, episode 203

### Run hiring like a sales funnel

- **What it is:** Treat finding staff the way you treat finding customers. Get applicants, keep them warm, interview them, and move fast.
- **How to use it:**
  1. Treat the job title like a headline. She searched Google and read about 17 similar job posts to pick a title that draws the right level of person.
  2. Skip long tests. Strong candidates with other offers will not take them. Use conversations and situational interviews instead.
  3. Book the next step while you are still on the call, and follow up the same day
  4. In a small company, the CEO or owner does the last interview
- **Source:** Young and Profiting podcast, episode 203

### Competency maps

- **What it is:** A simple written career ladder for each role. It shows the skills needed to move up, how pay changes, and who manages whom.
- **How to use it:**
  1. Start with three levels: does the work, manages people, leads managers
  2. For each level, list the skills and habits needed to get there
  3. Share it with the team so they can see a future with you
- **Source:** Young and Profiting podcast, episode 203

### Hard conversations: the 6 Es and 4 As

- **What it is:** A two part script for tough talks with an employee. This comes from a fan recap of her episode 48, not from her own written notes.
- **How to use it:**
  1. Before they speak (6 Es): set a serious tone, make clear what is at stake, explain the goal, explain where the issue came from, show the impact, and rate how serious it is from 1 to 10
  2. Then (4 As): ask for their side, advise on a fix, agree on a plan and check in times, and assure them where they stand
  3. Do it one on one, in person or on video
- **Source:** Podup recap of "Build with Leila Hormozi" episode 48

### Right person, right seat

- **What it is:** When someone is struggling, ask if it is the wrong seat (wrong role), the wrong season (bad timing in their life), or the wrong boss. The goal is the right people in the right seats, not keeping everyone. More effort or coaching will not fix a placement problem.
- **How to use it:**
  1. Name the problem in one sentence
  2. Test each of the three causes
  3. Move the person, give them time, change the manager, or let them go
- **Source:** "Right People, Right Seat", Build with Leila Hormozi, September 2, 2026 (episode description)

## Key principles

- If the team is not delivering, look at accountability first, before you add people or ads
- Vague feedback can sound like praise. Be clear and specific.
- Feedback gets easier with practice. Give it often so it feels normal.
- Speed wins in hiring. She says top candidates get a job in about eight days.
- Your reputation online is part of hiring. Candidates read your reviews.
- Doing more rarely fixes a broken business. Often the fix is to remove something.
- More effort, hours, or coaching will not fix a person in the wrong seat.
- A big vision attracts strong people. A small one does not.

## Mindset

- She calls herself the "chief accountability officer." The leader's job is to give people a clear reading of where they stand.
- Act on what you have been avoiding. She suggests listing five things you have put off and doing them tomorrow.
- Move quickly after a failure instead of dwelling on it
- People are judged by what they do, not what they meant to do
- In hard times, ask more of the team, but be open about why (Jasmine Star ep 337).
- Ask daily: does my team have support, and do they have clarity?

## Strategies and plays

- **Retitle a stuck job post.** Example: "administrative assistant" became "senior administrative assistant" and drew people with years more experience.
- **Score every role 1 to 10.** Tell people where they stand, for example that they are at a 7, and what it takes to reach the next number.
- **Ask values questions in interviews.** Example: "Tell me about the last hard conversation you had and how it went."
- **Ask for numbers.** Example: "Tell me about a time you reduced churn." Churn means customers who leave. Then ask for the numbers.
- **Listen to how leaders talk.** Someone who mostly talks about their status is a warning sign. Someone who talks about clients and the team is a good sign.
- **Be honest about growth.** Do not promise a big career path the role cannot give.
- **Subtract before you add.** Example: before buying a new tool, remove one step nobody needs.
- **Weigh big decisions.** Ask how many people it touches, how often it gets reused, how long it lasts, and whether you can undo it.

## Tools they use or recommend

She talks about methods more than software. Few specific tools are known. These are the ones found in sources:

- **Google, Salary.com, Payscale.com:** for checking job titles and pay for a role (Young and Profiting, episode 203)
- **A 1 to 10 scorecard:** a simple rating for where someone stands in their role (Young and Profiting, episode 203)
- **Job boards like Indeed, LinkedIn, Glassdoor, Monster, AngelList, plus a hiring page on your own site:** named in a third party write up of her hiring process (ProcessDriven), so treat as secondhand
- **Acquisition.com free courses and her newsletter, Leila's Letters:** her own free learning material (acquisition.com, leilahormozi.com)

## Questions they would ask you

Use these to pressure test a plan.

1. What does "good" look like for this role, in writing?
2. How do you measure it, and does the person know their score today?
3. When did you last give this person clear, direct feedback?
4. Is this the wrong person, the wrong seat, the wrong season, or the wrong boss?
5. What problem in the business will this hire solve?
6. How fast can you go from first call to offer?
7. What decision still has to go through you that someone else could make?
8. What could you remove instead of adding something new?
9. What hard conversation have you been putting off?
10. Can your team see where they could be in two years with you?

## Watch out

- Her lessons come from fast growing companies with large teams. A one person shop may not need career ladders or four interview rounds yet.
- Some of her frameworks reach us through podcast summaries and fan recaps. Check the episode before you treat a detail as her exact method.
- "Move fast" and "fire fast" can turn harsh if you skip the clear expectations and feedback that are supposed to come first
- Hiring for traits over skills is risky for roles that need a license or deep technical skill on day one
- Scoring people 1 to 10 only helps if the scale is written down and fair. A secret or shifting scale hurts trust.
- Her advice is about running a team. It is not a substitute for employment law advice on hiring or firing.
