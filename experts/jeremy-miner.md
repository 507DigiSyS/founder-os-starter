# Jeremy Miner

> A summary of this person's public teachings, written in our own words. It is not written or endorsed by them. Check the sources before you quote anything.

## Who they are

Jeremy Miner is a sales trainer. He founded 7th Level, a sales training company, and created a method called NEPQ (Neuro-Emotional Persuasion Questioning). He started in door to door home security sales, and later co-wrote the book *The New Model of Selling: Selling to an Unsellable Generation* with Jerry Acuff (Morgan James Publishing, 2023).

## Use this expert when

- You are getting ready for a sales call and want better questions
- Your close rate is low and people say "I need to think about it"
- Prospects go cold after a call or a proposal
- Your sales script sounds pushy, salesy or scripted
- You want to train a small team to run calls the same way
- You are writing follow up emails or DMs and want them to sound calm, not needy

## Core frameworks

### NEPQ (Neuro-Emotional Persuasion Questioning)

- **What it is:** a way of selling with questions, so the buyer talks themselves into the change instead of you pushing them.
- **How to use it:**
  1. Stop pitching early. Ask questions instead.
  2. Ask them in a set order (see the stages below).
  3. Use a calm, neutral tone, so the buyer gets curious, not defensive.
  4. Let the buyer say the problem and the cost of the problem in their own words.
- **Source:** 7th Level home page, Jeremy Miner team page (full name), and the Young and Profiting interview

### The five stages of a sales call

- **What it is:** one call moves through five stages, from Connecting to Commitment.
- **How to use it:**
  1. **Connecting:** put the focus on them, not you. Help them lower their guard.
  2. **Engagement:** ask questions about their life now, their problems, what they want, and what happens if nothing changes.
  3. **Transition:** repeat back what you heard, in their words, then bridge into your offer.
  4. **Presentation:** show only the parts of your offer that fix the problems they named.
  5. **Commitment:** ask for a clear next step. That might be a purchase, or just the next meeting.
- **Source:** 7th Level NEPQ 2.0 page (five stages, Connection through Commitment) and the Website Closers podcast write-up (connecting, transition, presentation and commitment). The label "Engagement" for the middle questioning stage is the common community name; his own pages do not spell it out.

### The question types, in order

- **What it is:** the order of questions inside the Engagement stage.
- **How to use it:**
  1. **Situation questions:** what is going on for them today.
  2. **Problem awareness questions:** what is not working, and how it makes them feel.
  3. **Solution awareness questions:** what life would look like once the problem is fixed.
  4. **Consequence questions:** what happens if they change nothing. This makes them argue for why they need to act.
- **Source:** Young and Profiting interview (E297)

### Three ways to sell

- **What it is:** his map of sales styles.
- **How to use it:** check which one you are doing now.
  1. **Pressure ("boiler room") selling:** push and pitch. Buyers resist.
  2. **Consultative selling:** logical, surface level questions. Buyers give vague answers and object late.
  3. **NEPQ (dialogue):** deeper, emotional questions. Buyers persuade themselves.
- **Source:** Young and Profiting interview (E297)

### Always Be Disarming

- **What it is:** his swap for "Always Be Closing." Keep lowering the buyer's guard instead of pushing for the close.
- **How to use it:** ask questions that make it safe to be honest, and act like a problem finder, not a pitch person.
- **Source:** Catapulting Commissions podcast, "Always Be Disarming"

## Key principles

- Find problems before you try to solve them. Sales is problem finding first.
- People decide with feelings, then explain it with logic.
- Buyers have heard so many pitches that they put up a wall the moment they sense one.
- Prevent objections early instead of fighting them at the end.
- Tone matters as much as the words. The same question can open a buyer up or shut them down.
- Listen for what the buyer means, not only what they say.
- Sales is a skill you learn, not a gift you are born with.
- People buy from someone they trust to get them the best result.

## Mindset

- **Detach from the sale.** You still want the sale, but you keep that to yourself. Buyers can feel need, and it pushes them away. He calls this "commission breath."
- **Stay neutral.** Not too excited, not timid. Calm and curious.
- **Confidence comes from skill.** Practice every day, and confidence follows.
- **Keep training.** Top performers in any field never stop practicing.
- **Status can be earned in the call.** You do not need fame. Good questions show you know your stuff.

## Strategies and plays

- **Say less, ask more.** Example: instead of explaining your service, ask "What made you look into this now?"
- **Use "no" questions.** People feel safer saying no. Example: "Would you be against looking at a few options?"
- **Play it down so they lean in.** He calls this mismatching: when you sound unsure it is a fit, buyers often argue for why it is. Example: "I am not sure this is even a fit for you."
- **Pause after a question.** Silence gives the buyer room to think and say more.
- **Ask about hesitation.** If you hear a weak "yeah, sure," gently ask what is behind it.
- **Ask the consequence question before price comes up.** Once they see the cost of doing nothing, price feels smaller.
- **Repeat their words back** before you present anything.
- **Kill the "just checking in" email.** Send a short, neutral note. He suggests ending with a question like "Where should we go from here?"
- **Walk through proposals live,** on screen, instead of emailing them and waiting.
- **Post short videos.** He suggests about one a day to build trust before the call.

## Questions they would ask you

Use these to pressure test a plan.

1. How much of your sales call is you talking? Is it more than a third?
2. What problem does the buyer say they have, in their own words?
3. What happens to them if they change nothing for six more months?
4. Where in your call do you sound needy or rushed?
5. Which objection do you hear most, and what earlier question would stop it?
6. Do you present your whole offer, or only the parts that fix what they named?
7. What exact next step do you ask for at the end of every call?
8. Are your follow up messages neutral, or do they say "just checking in"?
9. Are you practicing your calls every week, or just doing them?
10. Would a buyer feel safe telling you no?

## Watch out

- **Built for one on one calls.** A third party guide (11x, which sells AI sales software) says NEPQ fits best when you talk to a single decision maker. Deals with many buyers, committees or procurement need more.
- **It can feel like a trick if you fake it.** Questions asked with fake concern feel manipulative. Only ask what you truly want to know.
- **Do not use pain to pressure.** Consequence questions should help the buyer see clearly, not scare them into buying.
- **Do not copy scripts word for word.** Canned NEPQ lines can sound odd in your industry. Use your own words.
- **Results are self reported.** Many numbers on his site (people trained, close rates) come from his company or testimonials, not outside checks.
- **Originality debate.** A third party guide reports claims that NEPQ looks a lot like Michael Oliver's earlier "Natural Selling" method. Read both and decide for yourself.
- **It is a lot of practice.** The tone and pauses take time. Expect awkward calls at first.
- **Questions do not fix a bad offer.** If the offer is weak or priced wrong, fix that first.
