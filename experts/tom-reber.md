# Tom Reber

> A summary of this person's public teachings, written in our own words. It is not written or endorsed by them. Check the sources before you quote anything.

## Who they are

Tom Reber founded The Contractor Fight in 2014, a coaching and training company for owners of home service and trade businesses (painters, remodelers, roofers, landscapers). He has since handed the CEO role to someone else and remains its founder. He is a U.S. Marine Corps veteran. He co-owned a Chicago painting company that grew to 300 to 400 projects a year, and he sold his half in 2012. He hosts The Contractor Fight podcast, wrote *Winning the Contractor Fight* and *Sell Unafraid*, and later started Simplify Painting in Colorado Springs.

## Use this expert when

- You run a trade, home service, or project based business (any business that sends people out to do jobs)
- Your prices feel low, or you are busy but not making money
- You waste time driving to estimates for people who never buy
- You need to qualify leads (check if they are a good fit) before you meet them
- You are scared to say your price out loud
- You feel stuck in the business, doing every job yourself
- You want a simple way to find the one problem holding the business back

## Core frameworks

### The Fight OS (six fights)

- **What it is:** His way of splitting a business into six areas: Numbers, Marketing, Sales, Field (doing the work), Leadership, and Operating rhythm (your weekly routine of meetings and check ins).
- **How to use it:**
  1. Rate yourself in each of the six areas.
  2. Pick the one area that costs you the most money or time right now.
  3. Work only on that area until it is fixed.
  4. Then pick the next one.
- **Source:** [thecontractorfight.com](https://thecontractorfight.com)

### Shin-Fu sales process (phone first qualifying)

- **What it is:** A five step phone call you do before you ever visit a job site. It was built by Steve Shinholser, a coach at The Contractor Fight, and it is the core of their sales training.
- **How to use it:**
  1. **Motive:** Ask why they want the job done, and why now. Repeat their reason back to them.
  2. **Money:** Ask if you can share a typical price range for jobs like this, then stay quiet and listen. Find out if you are in the same range before you go out.
  3. **Truth:** Agree up front that surprises (like hidden rot) get talked about before the price changes.
  4. **Influencers:** Find out who else gets a say, like a spouse or landlord, and include them early.
  5. **BS Meter (consultation fee):** Charge for an in person visit (TCI Magazine's example is $150 for an arborist) to filter out people who are not serious.
- **Source:** [TCI Magazine](https://tcimag.tcia.org/?p=11723), [TCF850 episode](https://www.everand.com/podcast/694849386/TCF850-The-Psychology-Behind-the-Shin-Fu-You-must-have-come-across-many-prospects-who-agree-to-a-job-and-price-on-the-phone-but-then-back-out-or-mak), [Jobber podcast](https://getjobber.com/podcast/s2e27-tom-reber/)

### 50 percent gross profit target

- **What it is:** A pricing rule. Gross profit is the price minus the direct job costs (labor, materials, rentals, permits). He says aim for at least 50 percent on every job.
- **How to use it:**
  1. Add up the direct costs of the job. Say it is $3,000.
  2. Double it. The price should be no less than $6,000.
  3. The $3,000 left over pays your salary, office costs, marketing, and trucks.
  4. Note: doubling cost is a 100 percent markup, which gives a 50 percent margin. A 50 percent markup only gives about a 33 percent margin. Mixing these up is a common way to lose money.
- **Source:** [Hearth podcast with Tom Reber](https://gethearth.com/the-change-order-tom-reber/)

### Successful, miserable, trapped

- **What it is:** A way to name a common owner problem. The business looks good from the outside, but the owner is stuck in every decision and unhappy.
- **How to use it:**
  1. Ask: if I left for two weeks, what would break?
  2. List those things. Each one is a system or a person you still need.
  3. Fix one each month.
- **Source:** [thecontractorfight.com](https://thecontractorfight.com)

### Sell Unafraid

- **What it is:** His sales book. It covers four parts of selling: mindset, ethical selling, smart prospecting, and what happens after the sale.
- **How to use it:**
  1. Ask better questions to learn what matters to this customer.
  2. Say your price with confidence, with no hedging.
  3. Focus on closing the jobs that pay well, not every job.
- **Source:** [Sell Unafraid book page](https://thecontractorfight.com/sell-unafraid)

## Key principles

- The first fight is with yourself, not with cheap customers or competitors.
- Know your numbers. Learn job costing (tracking what each job really cost you).
- If customers cannot see why you are better, they will shop on price.
- Qualify on the phone. Do not drive to every estimate.
- Not every lead is a good client. Saying no is part of selling.
- Talk less, ask more. Skip the tech talk customers do not care about.
- The business should serve your life, not run it.
- Practice sales like a skill. Role play often.

## Mindset

- Many problems are self inflicted. He calls it "friendly fire": debt, overwork, negative self talk.
- Winning is a choice. Change how you see yourself first, then the business.
- Price from value, not from fear. Scarcity thinking makes you cut prices.
- Act like the expert. The customer called you because they need help. Be confident, not arrogant.
- Build discipline with small daily goals.
- Take care of yourself and your home first. His site uses the theme "Strong You. Strong Home. Strong Business."

## Strategies and plays

- **Phone screen every lead:** Ask for photos first, then do a short call with motive and money before any site visit.
- **Share a price range early:** For example (our wording): "Jobs like this usually run $8,000 to $12,000. Is that in the ballpark?"
- **Charge for the visit:** A paid consultation weeds out tire kickers.
- **Profit touch:** Reach out to past customers to get repeat work and referrals.
- **Pricing page:** Show typical price ranges on your website so no one is shocked. (This idea came from the Jobber host in his interview.)
- **Reinvest in marketing:** At Simplify Painting he said he put most of the leftover profit back into marketing.
- **Fix one fight at a time:** Do not try to fix sales, hiring, and marketing in the same month.

## Questions they would ask you

Use these to pressure test a plan.

1. What is your gross profit on your last 10 jobs, not your guess, the real number?
2. Do you know the difference between markup and margin? Show me the math.
3. Why does this customer want the job done, and why now?
4. Did you talk money before you drove out there?
5. Who else has a say in this decision, and were they in the room?
6. How many leads did you say no to last month? If zero, why?
7. If you left for two weeks, what would break?
8. Which of the six fights is costing you the most right now?
9. What does your week look like? Is there a set time for numbers, sales, and your team?
10. Are you fighting the market, or are you fighting yourself?

## Watch out

- **Built for trades.** His advice is made for jobs done at a customer's home. A software or online business will need to adapt it.
- **50 percent is a rule of thumb.** Some trades, markets, or large jobs may run lower. Check your own overhead before you copy the number.
- **Phone screening can push people away.** Some customers want you to see the job first. Talking money too early can feel cold if you skip the motive step.
- **Consultation fees do not fit every market.** In a market where everyone gives free estimates, a fee may cut good leads too.
- **Mindset is not a fix for bad math.** "The fight is with yourself" is a useful idea, but it does not replace real numbers.
- **He sells coaching.** Much of his free content leads to paid programs. His site says it makes no guarantees about results or earnings and that money figures are only examples.
- **Shin-Fu was built by a coach on his team,** not by Tom himself. Credit it correctly.
